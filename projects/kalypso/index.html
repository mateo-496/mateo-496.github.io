Kalypso business simulation

Ranked 1st of 11 teams on the simulator's combined scorecards, running a virtual boat manufacturer as CEO.

Ranked 1st of 11 teams on the simulator’s combined scorecards after three of the four quarters, as CEO of a virtual boat manufacturer.

  • Context: Kalypso business game (Arkhe), Arts et Métiers ParisTech, Aix-en-Provence, November 2023. Team of five.
  • Stack: Kalypso simulator, Excel (intermediate management balances, functional balance sheet), French national sector benchmarks.
  • Result: 1st of 11 on combined scorecards; revenue roughly doubled year on year to €8.3M; return on equity up from about 5% to about 35%.

Kalypso is a turn-based business simulation. Each team takes over the same small company, which builds three boat models (A, B, C), and makes one set of decisions per quarter on prices, production, purchasing, staffing, advertising, credit terms and loans. Every quarter, the simulator scores each company out of 20 on five axes: profitability, cash, customer satisfaction, workshop management and raw-material stock management. Each score compares the company with fixed targets and with its competitors.

Strategy

The core idea was to undercut the market slightly, never heavily. Every quarter, the team read the competitors’ prices from the market study and set each boat a few percent below the average. That was enough to win buyers without giving the margin away.

  • Prices below the average on every boat, every quarter. The gap was 5–8% in Q1 and 6–10% in Q2. In Q3 it widened to 11–21%, with the lowest prices in the market on boats B and C.
  • Volume to match the prices. Low prices only pay if the boats are in stock. Production was pushed to capacity before the spring peak, and extra boat A stock was bought from another team.
  • Heavy advertising. From Q2 onwards, the monthly advertising budget was €60–65k: €30k on brand advertising, plus €5–20k per boat. The market study puts a normal budget at about €24k a month (€17k brand, €7.2k across the three boats), so the team spent roughly 2.5 times that, the largest advertising budget of the 11 teams. The brand budget alone was already well above normal, and in Q2 and Q3 boat A got ten times its recommended €1k.
  • Credit terms as a second lever. Customer payment terms were extended to attract buyers, then shortened when production costs threatened the cash balance. The company never went into overdraft.
  • A shift to margin late in the year. The mix moved towards boat C, the highest-margin model. The sales force grew by six, with higher pay and commission, and a new machine was bought for the last quarter.

Against the other teams

The pricing translated directly into market share. The team ranked 2nd of 11 on volume share in Q2 and 1st in Q3, with 24% of the boat A market.

The team also led the simulator’s combined scorecards in every quarter. It was 1st overall, 1st on profitability and 1st on cash.

Results

945 boats were sold over the year. Compared with the year before the takeover, revenue doubled to €8.27M, value added nearly tripled to €3.23M, and net profit rose from €0.16M to €1.08M.
